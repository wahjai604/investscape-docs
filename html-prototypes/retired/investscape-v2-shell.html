Lighthouse Research Ltd. — v2 Shell Concept

The InvestScape shell, version two: more doors, more control, one ecosystem.

Four screens introducing the platform-wide systems: the two-tier ribbon with collapsible search and Development Studio (Enterprise), the property category system with prospect-to-owned flow and portfolio filtering, the Library rendering the Commercial Formula Library (doc 06) as learning cards, and Community — Bullboards for real estate. Plus three ambient systems on every screen: Customize mode (drag & lock), the draggable AI bubble with voice input, and the language switcher.

Everything interactive here works: click the search icon to expand it, toggle Customize to drag KPI cards, filter the portfolio by category, hide rows with the eye icon, open the export menu, switch languages with the globe (nav and KPI labels translate live — EN / FR / 繁體 / 简体), and drag the gold AI bubble anywhere. Figma imports static frames, so when you bring this into Buddy, capture key states (search open, customize on, AI panel open) as separate frames.

Capitalization Rate — the IRV triangle
F-404
R = I ÷ V  ·  V = I ÷ R  ·  I = V × R
One relationship, three uses: measure a deal's unleveraged yield, price a building from its income, or infer what income a price implies. InvestScape shows two cap rates on every deal — market cap (NOI ÷ price) and yield-on-cost — and never conflates them.
NOI $106,000 on a $2,500,000 building → R = 4.24%. At a 4% market cap, that income supports V = $2,650,000.

Debt Service Coverage Ratio
F-502
DSCR = NOI ÷ Annual Debt Service
The lender's stress question: how many times over does the building's income cover the mortgage? 1.25 is the common commercial floor. Below 1.0, the property can't carry its own loan.
NOI $81,000 against $64,800 of annual payments → DSCR 1.25, exactly at a typical lender minimum.
CommercialLoan Sizing card
Leverage State
F-505
cost of debt < unlevered yield → positive leverage
The mathematics behind "cheap debt makes deals." When the building yields more than the loan costs, borrowing concentrates the spread onto a smaller equity base and raises the equity return. When rates exceed yields, the same arithmetic runs in reverse.
A 6.0% building financed at 4.5% interest-only: $300K of equity earns 9.0%. The same building at a 7.5% rate: equity earns just 3.0%.
CommercialAI narrative
Net Operating Income — the waterfall
F-401
PRI − vacancy + other income − OpEx = NOI
What the building itself earns before any financing or tax effects — the single most important number on the platform. Debt service, depreciation, capex, reserves, and the owner's income taxes are all excluded; a seller's "NOI" that blurs these lines is the most common source of an overstated cap rate.
12 units at $1,650/mo plus parking, 4% vacancy, $84,000 expenses → NOI $159,312.

Net Present Value
F-410
NPV = Σ [CFₜ ÷ (1+r)ᵗ] − Initial Investment
At your required rate of return, is this deal worth more than it costs — and by how many dollars? Positive means it beats your hurdle; zero means it exactly meets it. Better than IRR for ranking deals of different sizes, because it answers in dollars rather than percentages.
Cash flows worth $1.40M today against a $1.23M investment → NPV +$167K at a 15% required return.

Present Value of an Annuity
F-205
PV = PMT × [(1 − (1+i)⁻ⁿ) ÷ i]
Today's value of a stream of level payments — which makes it the loan formula in reverse: a mortgage is a lender buying your payment stream at a price that yields the contract rate. This one formula powers loan payments, remaining balances, and maximum-loan sizing.
$5,400/mo for 25 years at 5.2% (CA semi-annual compounding) prices at about $910,600 — the DSCR-method maximum loan.
