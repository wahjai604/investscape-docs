# Addendum: Correcting the Demand-Sizing Framing

*Supplements: "InvestScape: A Decision-Grade Strategy Report for a Solo, Bootstrapped Founder" (Strand 3 and Synthesis)*

## What the prior framing got wrong

The strategy report treated 1,000 paying customers as "ambitious but not impossible," to be managed down to a first milestone of 20–50 pre-paying believers before broader confidence was warranted. That was based on a misreading — you were saying the opposite of what I heard. You already have real evidence, not a hope:

- A basic Excel mortgage/rent calculator — no charts, no visualization, no SaaS polish — generated spontaneous requests for copies from attendees at an educational workshop with your former brokerage last year.
- Showcasing the InvestScape ecosystem hub to your AI meetup group a few weeks ago produced strong positive feedback and multiple interested parties, including one wanting co-ownership, several asking about investing, and one offering to introduce you to a developer client in Arizona once you have a working MVP or pre-MVP.
- Your combined realtor, developer, and investor network across multiple networking groups easily exceeds 1,000 people.

This is a materially different starting position than "unproven demand for a niche product." You have organic pull-demand for the *underlying calculation* (people wanted the spreadsheet before any product existed) and validated interest in the *packaged ecosystem* (real people offering money, partnership, and warm introductions off a vibe-coded demo). That's not speculative market sizing — it's already-observed signal.

## What this changes

**The purpose of the focus groups shifts.** In the original framing, focus groups existed to find out *whether* people would pay. Given your actual evidence, that question is largely answered — the open question is *whether the product holds up at the volume you're already positioned to reach.* Focus groups now serve a different function: catching UX confusion, calculation-trust issues, and onboarding friction *before* you widen the funnel to a network that already wants in, so that a rushed or buggy launch doesn't burn goodwill you've already earned.

**The "first 20–50 believers" milestone was too conservative as a validation gate.** You don't need to re-prove basic willingness-to-pay; you need to convert already-warm interest carefully. The risk isn't "will anyone pay" — it's sequencing: launching too early to a network of 1,000+ people who are already primed to try it, and having the calculation engine or UX fail to hold up under real scrutiny from sophisticated realtors and investors who will check your numbers.

**1,000 paying customers should be treated as a realistic near-term target, not a stretch goal**, conditional on the product being ready when you open the doors — which raises the stakes on the engine-correctness and demo-labelling recommendations in the main report, not lowers them. The better-than-expected demand signal is a reason to be *more* careful about shipping correct math and honest labelling, not less, because you'll have more people testing it sooner.

## What doesn't change

The core sequencing logic still holds: build the engine correctly (Strand 2), label genuinely-functional vs. illustrative engines honestly for anyone you demo to — including the interested investors and the Arizona developer introduction (Strand 3's securities-law and misrepresentation guidance applies with more force now that real investment interest exists, not less), and get the co-founder question resolved deliberately (Strand 4) before any of this momentum turns into equity decisions made under time pressure.

One specific point worth flagging given the *already-expressed* investor interest: because you now have real people who've said they want to invest, the BC Securities Act guidance in the original report (misrepresentation, s.50/s.50(3)) is not a hypothetical future concern — it applies to your next conversation with them. Make sure whatever you show them next is accurately labelled for what's live versus illustrative.
