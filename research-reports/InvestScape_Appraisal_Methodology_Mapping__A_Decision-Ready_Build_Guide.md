# Appraisal Methodology Mapping for InvestScape: A Decision-Ready Build Guide

## TL;DR
- **Bake the income and development methods in now, defer the comparison/cost approaches, and never call any output an "appraisal."** InvestScape's existing income and development formula engines already implement the most defensible, data-light appraisal methods (direct capitalization, band of investment, DCF/yield capitalization, cost-approach arithmetic); these should be extended in the Bubble MVP. The direct comparison approach and market-extracted cap rates should be deferred to Route 2 because the free v1 data stack cannot supply verified comparable sales.
- **The subdivision development method is the single highest-value addition and directly fills three logged Dev Studio gaps** (absorption/sales pace, unit mix, take-out financing) — it is a discounted-cash-flow land-valuation technique that fits InvestScape's Full Proforma natively and differentiates it from generic residential calculators.
- **Regulatory posture is the binding constraint, not methodology.** In Alberta the term "real estate appraiser" is statutorily protected; CUSPAP governs anyone who produces a "value"; and Eric's active BCFSA licence raises misrepresentation exposure. Every valuation-adjacent output must use "estimate/analytical output" framing modelled on the Zestimate/Redfin/HouseSigma "not an appraisal" disclaimers, with the AI layer describing computed numbers only.

## Key Findings

1. **The three approaches to value are the organizing spine, but they are not equally suited to a data-light SaaS.** The income approach (direct capitalization and yield capitalization/DCF) maps cleanly onto InvestScape's existing commercial and development engines and requires only user-entered or free-feed data. The cost approach is arithmetically simple but needs construction-cost data and depreciation judgment. The direct (sales) comparison approach is the residential gold standard but is the most data-hungry — it requires a licensed comparable-sales feed InvestScape has deferred.

2. **InvestScape already implements more appraisal methodology than the founder may realize.** NOI reconstruction, cap-rate capitalization (V = NOI/R), DSCR, GRM, band of investment, and DCF with reversion are all textbook income-approach techniques. The platform's commercial formula library is effectively a partial income-approach engine already; the work is framing and completing it, not inventing it.

3. **The subdivision development method and land residual technique are the appraisal methods most aligned with Dev Studio** and are already 60–70% latent in the development formula addendum (land, hard/soft costs, financing, absorption basics). Completing them closes deferred gaps rather than adding scope.

4. **"Appraisal" is a protected activity in Canada, and the risk is real for a licensee.** CUSPAP 2024 (effective January 1, 2024), Definition 3.6 "Appraisal Practice," Note 2 states verbatim: *"The use of other terms (e.g., analysis, counselling, evaluation, study, submission, valuation) does not exempt adherence to these Standards. To avoid confusion, terminology included in CUSPAP should be used."* Alberta's Real Estate Act (s. 17.1) makes it an offence to act as, advertise as, or hold oneself out as a real estate appraiser without membership in a recognized body (AIC, Alberta Assessors' Association, or CNAREA). Eric's BCFSA licence imposes a statutory duty to act honestly and with reasonable care and skill that extends to his conduct broadly.

5. **Every major AVM disclaims itself out of "appraisal" status using near-identical language.** Zillow's "What is a Zestimate?" page states verbatim: *"It is not an appraisal and can't be used in place of an appraisal. It is a computer-generated estimate of the value of a home today, given the available data"* (Zillow publishes Zestimates for 110M+ homes). Redfin's disclaimer: *"The Redfin Estimate is not a formal appraisal or substitute for the in-person expertise of a real estate agent or professional appraiser."* HouseSigma and Zolo use "starting point," "not an official appraisal." This is the template InvestScape should follow.

6. **The 2024 federal AVM rule does not directly bind InvestScape but signals the direction of travel.** Per the Federal Register final rule (Docket CFPB-2023-0025, published August 7, 2024, effective October 1, 2025), six agencies — the OCC, Federal Reserve Board, FDIC, NCUA, CFPB, and FHFA — jointly adopted quality-control and non-discrimination standards for AVMs used by mortgage originators and secondary-market issuers in credit decisions and covered securitization determinations. The rule defines an AVM as *"any computerized model used by mortgage originators and secondary market issuers to determine the value of a consumer's principal dwelling collateralizing a mortgage."* InvestScape is neither an originator nor an issuer, so it is out of scope today — but the rule shows regulators now scrutinize automated valuation, and the non-discrimination principle should inform the Neighbourhood Intel "numbers-only" guardrail.

## Details

### A. WHERE each method fits in InvestScape

**Income approach — direct capitalization → Deal Analyzer (commercial), already built, extend.**
Direct capitalization converts a single stabilized year of income into value: **Value = NOI ÷ Overall Capitalization Rate (Ro)**. NOI is reconstructed as: Potential Gross Income − Vacancy & Collection Loss + Other Income = Effective Gross Income; EGI − Operating Expenses = NOI (excluding debt service, income tax, depreciation, and capital expenditures). The operating expense ratio (OER = OpEx ÷ EGI) and break-even ratio are supporting outputs. This is the core of InvestScape's commercial library. The one methodologically important addition is **cap-rate derivation**, which has two forms:
- **Market extraction:** Ro = NOI ÷ Sale Price from comparable sales. Requires comparable NOI and price — data InvestScape does not have in v1. Defer.
- **Band of investment (mortgage-equity):** Ro = (M × Rm) + ((1 − M) × Re), where M = loan-to-value ratio, Rm = mortgage constant (annual debt service ÷ loan principal), Re = equity dividend rate. This needs only user inputs and the mortgage constant InvestScape already computes. **Build this now** — it lets users derive a cap rate without a comps feed, a genuine differentiator.

**Income approach — yield capitalization / DCF → Deal Analyzer (commercial) + Dev Studio Full Proforma, already partially built.**
DCF discounts a multi-year cash-flow stream plus a reversion to present value:
**Value = Σ [NOIₜ ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ] for t = 1…N, plus Reversionₙ ÷ (1 + r)ᴺ**, where r = discount rate (yield rate), N = holding period. The **reversion (terminal) value = forward NOI in year N+1 ÷ terminal (exit) cap rate**. The terminal cap rate is conventionally set modestly above the going-in cap rate to reflect the asset's greater age and forecast uncertainty at disposition. Discount-rate selection should be surfaced as an explicit, user-set assumption with sensitivity analysis, because a small change in the exit cap rate can move value materially. InvestScape's IRR/NPV coverage means this is largely present; the framing addition is labelling it "yield capitalization" in the Library and making reversion/terminal-cap-rate first-class inputs.

**Gross income multipliers → Deal Analyzer (residential + commercial), simple add.**
GRM = Sale Price ÷ Gross (Monthly or Annual) Rent; PGIM and EGIM are the potential- and effective-gross variants. These are screening ratios, not value conclusions, and are trivial to compute. Good for the residential Deal Analyzer as a quick-filter metric.

**Cost approach → Deal Analyzer (new construction) + Library, moderate build.**
**Property Value = Land Value + (Cost New − Accrued Depreciation)**. Cost New is either **reproduction cost** (exact replica, period materials) or **replacement cost** (equivalent utility, current materials/standards); replacement cost is the practical default. Accrued depreciation aggregates three forms: **physical deterioration** (wear), **functional obsolescence** (outdated design/superadequacy), and **external/economic obsolescence** (off-site causes). The **economic age-life method** is the implementable default: **Accrued Depreciation = (Effective Age ÷ Total Economic Life) × Cost New**. The breakdown and market-extraction depreciation methods are more accurate but need more data/judgment — keep them as Library entries only. The cost approach is most reliable for new construction and special-use property and least reliable for older buildings.

**Direct (sales) comparison approach → Deal Analyzer + Neighbourhood Intel, DEFER to Route 2.**
The methodology: select comparables similar in property rights, financing terms, conditions of sale, market conditions (time), location, and physical characteristics; then adjust each comp to the subject via a market-data grid, applying adjustments in the correct sequence (transactional adjustments — property rights, financing, conditions of sale, market conditions — before property adjustments). **Paired sales analysis** derives each adjustment by comparing two sales differing in one feature. This is the most rigorous residential method — but it is entirely dependent on verified comparable-sales data, which the free v1 stack (FRED, Bank of Canada, CMHC, Redfin CSVs, municipal parcel assessments) cannot supply at parcel level with reliable transaction detail. Building an adjustment grid without a comps feed would produce unsupported outputs — exactly the liability InvestScape must avoid. Defer until a licensed feed (MLS/DDF, post-regulatory-counsel) exists.

**Highest and best use → Dev Studio + Library, framing + light calculation.**
The four tests: **legally permissible, physically possible, financially feasible, and maximally productive.** The first two are screening filters (zoning, physical site constraints). Financial feasibility is a proforma test (a use is feasible if its NPV > 0). Maximal productivity ranks the feasible uses by value or risk-adjusted return (highest residual land value / IRR / NPV). This maps directly onto Dev Studio: the Full Proforma already computes the feasibility and productivity tests numerically. Present HBU as a structured Dev Studio workflow plus a Library explainer — not as a value conclusion.

**Land valuation techniques → Dev Studio, the highest-value cluster.**
Six techniques: direct comparison, extraction, allocation, land residual, ground rent capitalization, and the **subdivision development method**. The subdivision development method is a DCF applied to land: estimate gross sale proceeds of the finished lots/units, subtract all development costs (hard, soft, financing, marketing, commissions) and developer's profit/entrepreneurial incentive, phase the sell-out over an **absorption period**, and discount the net cash flows to present value to derive land value. This is functionally identical to what Dev Studio's Full Proforma does — so it directly fills the logged deferred gaps of **absorption/sales pace, unit mix, and take-out/permanent financing**. The **land residual technique** (Land Value = Income to Land ÷ Land Cap Rate, after deducting income attributable to the building) and **building residual technique** are income-approach tools useful for the HBU "as vacant vs as improved" test. The general residual model developers know as **Residual Land Value = Gross Development Value − (Construction + Fees + Profit)** should be the headline Dev Studio "what can I pay for the land?" output.

**Reconciliation → Deal Analyzer + Library, framing rule.**
Reconciliation is the appraiser's weighting of multiple value indications into a single conclusion. InvestScape should NOT auto-reconcile into a single "value" (that edges toward appraisal). Instead, present each approach's indication side by side with the assumptions, and let the user judge — an "analytical output," not a reconciled opinion of value.

**Portfolio tab:** roll-up of per-asset income-approach metrics (aggregate NOI, DSCR at portfolio/"global" level, weighted cap rate, equity position). No new appraisal method required.

**Neighbourhood Intel:** statistical/market context only. Market and marketability analysis (supply/demand, absorption, demographic and economic drivers) belongs here as *measured statistics* — never as AI lifestyle/demographic inference, consistent with the existing guardrail and the AVM non-discrimination principle.

**Library/Glossary:** home for every method's plain-language explainer, the CUSPAP/USPAP terminology, the three-forms-of-depreciation taxonomy, HBU four tests, and the Canadian-vs-US methodology notes.

### B. Canadian-specific considerations

- **CUSPAP is the governing standard** (2024 edition, effective January 1, 2024, revised every two years) published by the Appraisal Institute of Canada. It defines "appraisal practice" as six regulated activities (Real Property Appraisal, Review, Consulting, Reserve Fund Study, Machinery and Equipment Appraisal, and Mass Appraisal) and — critically — states that using other terms does not exempt work from the Standards. Compliance is mandatory for AIC members; InvestScape is not an AIC member and must not represent CUSPAP compliance.
- **Designations:** AACI (Accredited Appraiser Canadian Institute) — all property types; CRA (Canadian Residential Appraiser) — residential up to four self-contained units and undeveloped residential sites. These are the only bodies that produce certified market-value opinions.
- **Semi-annual vs monthly compounding** (already implemented): Canadian fixed mortgages compound semi-annually not in advance by law; US mortgages compound monthly. This is the correct, FCAC-consistent basis and directly affects the mortgage constant used in band of investment and DSCR — so the dual-market toggle already feeds the appraisal math correctly.
- **GST/HST treatment (Dev Studio-critical):** New residential construction and presale condos are subject to **5% federal GST**; used/resale residential is generally GST-exempt. GST is usually **additional to** the quoted price (e.g., about $25,000 on a $500,000 new home) and is typically payable at completion, not contract signing. The **GST/HST New Housing Rebate** returns 36% of the federal GST to a maximum of $6,300; per the Parliamentary Budget Officer (report RP-2526-001-S), the rebate *"is linearly phased out for houses valued between $350,000 and $450,000, and there is no rebate for houses valued at $450,000 or more"* (phase-out: Rebate = $6,300 × ($450,000 − Price) ÷ $100,000). A newly enacted **First-Time Home Buyers' GST Rebate** — Bill C-4, which received Royal Assent March 12, 2026 with provisions deemed in force retroactively to March 20, 2025 — eliminates GST for first-time buyers on new homes: per PwC Canada Tax Insights, for homes *"$1 million or less, the rebate will equal 100% of the GST or federal portion of the HST (saving first-time homebuyers up to $50,000)"*, phased out linearly to zero at $1.5 million (e.g., a $1.25 million home yields a $25,000 rebate). In a development proforma, **land purchased by a corporation/partnership is generally GST-taxable, and hard/soft construction-cost GST is recoverable by a registered developer as input tax credits** — so proforma costs should be modelled net of recoverable GST, while GST is collected on and remitted from sale proceeds. Note the CRA now instructs its appraisers to report fair market value of newly constructed residential as **GST/HST-inclusive** (tax "backed out"), because exempt resale comparables would not command a price plus GST. Statutorily, FMV itself excludes GST/HST payable. Dev Studio should therefore let users toggle GST-inclusive vs GST-exclusive pricing and flag recoverable ITCs — a concrete gap to log.
- **Assessment vs market appraisal:** BC Assessment (and MPAC in Ontario) use **mass appraisal** — computer-assisted valuation of large property groups as of a statutory date (July 1 of the prior year for BC), for **tax distribution**, not transaction pricing. Assessed value routinely diverges from market value. InvestScape uses municipal parcel assessments as a free data input — these must be labelled as assessed (tax) values, explicitly NOT market value or appraisal.

### C. VALUE each brings to the SaaS

- **Income approach (built):** table-stakes credibility with investor/broker segments; the band of investment addition lets users derive a cap rate without a comps feed — a real differentiator versus generic calculators.
- **Subdivision development method / land residual (Dev Studio):** the strongest differentiator. Few consumer tools model absorption-phased DCF land valuation; it speaks directly to Eric's developer segment and is a natural premium/upgrade-tier feature.
- **Cost approach (age-life):** cheap credibility for new-construction and insurance-replacement contexts; low build cost.
- **HBU framework:** positions Dev Studio as an analysis environment, not just a calculator; strong educational/Library draw for realtor and novice-investor segments.
- **Direct comparison (deferred):** would be high-value for the realtor/residential segment but is gated on data licensing; its absence is not a credibility gap if the income and development tools are strong.
- **Upgrade-tier logic:** free tier = screening ratios (GRM, cap rate, DSCR, cash-on-cash); paid tier = full DCF, subdivision development model, equity waterfall, sensitivity analysis, portfolio roll-up.

### D. DISADVANTAGES

- **Bubble.io complexity:** multi-year DCF, S-curve construction draws, absorption phasing, and equity waterfalls are iterative/array-heavy calculations that strain a no-code environment. Bubble can do them but maintainability and performance degrade with complexity — a strong argument for keeping the heaviest math (subdivision DCF, waterfall) minimal in Route 1 and rebuilding cleanly in Postgres/React for Route 2.
- **Data the free stack cannot satisfy:** verified comparable sales (kills the direct comparison approach and market-extracted cap rates at parcel level); construction-cost multipliers for the cost approach (Cost New needs a costing source such as a published cost manual); market rent and expense benchmarks for NOI reconstruction (users must supply these). Every method whose inputs the platform cannot verify pushes valuation responsibility onto the user — which is legally protective but limits "one-click" value.
- **Maintenance burden:** cap-rate conventions, GST thresholds, and rebate rules change (e.g., the 2025–2026 FTHB rebate); these need a maintainable assumptions table, not hard-coded constants.
- **User-comprehension burden:** DCF discount-rate and terminal-cap-rate selection is where non-expert users go wrong. Guardrails, tooltips, and Library explainers are mandatory, and the AI layer must explain—not choose—these assumptions.

### E. PUSHBACKS (legal/regulatory)

- **Do not produce a single reconciled "value" or anything labelled "appraisal," "appraised value," or "valuation."** CUSPAP's own text closes the "call it something else" loophole. Use "estimate," "analytical output," "the numbers indicate," "projected," "modelled."
- **Alberta term protection:** the Real Estate Act (s. 17.1) restricts acting as / holding out as a "real estate appraiser." A national SaaS reaching Alberta users must avoid the term entirely in product and marketing.
- **Founder's BCFSA licensee status is the sharpest exposure.** Eric owes statutory duties of honesty and reasonable care and skill; a valuation-adjacent tool that a consumer relies on and that proves misleading could rebound on his licence via misrepresentation/conduct-unbecoming exposure, independent of corporate liability. This argues for conservative framing, prominent disclaimers, and keeping the tool "analytical" and user-driven. Confirm with the pending real estate regulatory counsel and the E&O broker (AI liability rider).
- **AVM regulation:** the 2024 interagency final rule does not bind InvestScape today (not a mortgage originator/secondary-market issuer), but its non-discrimination and quality-control principles are the direction of travel — the Neighbourhood Intel "numbers-only, no demographic inference" rule is well-aligned and should be preserved.
- **Methods to exclude entirely (v1):** automated single-value residential "what's my home worth" outputs (Zestimate territory — highest confusion/liability, no data to support them); any auto-reconciled opinion of value; any output that ingests municipal assessed values and re-labels them as market value.
- **Scope-creep discipline:** the direct comparison approach is the classic scope-creep trap — it feels essential but is data-gated and liability-heavy. Park it behind the same gate as MLS/DDF access.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Now, in the Bubble MVP (extend existing engines, low incremental risk):**
1. Formalize and label the **income approach**: direct capitalization (V = NOI/R) with full NOI reconstruction (PGI → vacancy/collection loss → EGI → OpEx → NOI), OER and break-even outputs. Already ~built.
2. Add **band of investment** cap-rate derivation (Ro = M·Rm + (1−M)·Re) using the existing mortgage constant — this is the marquee "derive a cap rate without comps" feature.
3. Promote **DCF / yield capitalization** with explicit holding period, discount rate, reversion via terminal cap rate, and a sensitivity slider on the exit cap rate.
4. Add **GRM/PGIM/EGIM** screening ratios to the residential and commercial Deal Analyzer.
5. Complete the **subdivision development method** in Dev Studio Full Proforma — absorption phasing, unit mix, take-out financing — closing three logged deferred gaps. Surface **Residual Land Value = GDV − (costs + profit)** as the headline output.
6. Add a **GST/HST toggle** (inclusive/exclusive) and recoverable-ITC handling in Dev Studio; drive New Housing Rebate ($350K/$450K, $6,300 cap) and FTHB rebate ($1M/$1.5M, $50K cap) thresholds from a maintainable assumptions table.
7. Ship **HBU as a structured Dev Studio workflow** (four tests) + Library explainer.
8. Implement the **disclaimer/framing layer** platform-wide (see below) before any valuation-adjacent output ships.

**Stage 2 — Phase 2 / Route 2 (Postgres/React, after data + counsel):**
9. **Cost approach** with age-life depreciation, once a construction-cost source is integrated (Library-only until then).
10. **Land/building residual techniques** for the HBU as-vacant/as-improved comparison.
11. **Direct comparison approach** with a market-data adjustment grid and paired-sales support — only after a licensed comparable-sales feed (MLS/DDF) is secured and regulatory counsel signs off.
12. Market-extracted cap rates (needs the same comps feed).

**Skip entirely (v1 and likely v2):** automated single-value residential home-value estimates; auto-reconciled opinions of value; any re-labelling of assessed values as market value.

**Benchmarks that change the sequencing:** securing a licensed comparable-sales feed + favourable regulatory-counsel opinion → promotes direct comparison and market extraction from "skip/defer" to "build." Integrating a construction-cost data source → promotes the cost approach to "build." A decision to serve mortgage/lending workflows → triggers full 2024 AVM-rule analysis before shipping.

**Recommended disclaimer/framing principles (not legal advice — confirm with counsel):**
- Every valuation-adjacent screen: a persistent, plain statement that outputs are **"analytical estimates, not an appraisal, and not a substitute for a professional appraisal by an AACI- or CRA-designated appraiser,"** mirroring the Zillow/Redfin/HouseSigma model.
- Use **"the numbers indicate / based on the assumptions you entered"** framing; never "the property is worth X."
- Label all assessed-value data as **tax-assessment values, not market value.**
- Keep the AI layer to **describing platform-computed numbers**; it must never compute, reconcile, select a discount/cap rate, or infer non-numeric neighbourhood characteristics.
- Present multiple approaches side-by-side with visible assumptions; **do not auto-reconcile** into one value.
- Add a founder-specific line acknowledging InvestScape is a software analysis tool and its outputs are not the regulated services of a real estate licensee or appraiser.

## Caveats
- This is a methodology mapping from public sources (CUSPAP 2024, AIC and US Appraisal Institute public materials, IAAO/assessment bodies, CRA, CMHC-adjacent sources, BCFSA, RECA, and reputable industry literature), not an extraction of the copyrighted *Appraisal of Real Estate, Fourth Canadian Edition*. Formulas are standard, non-copyrightable practice described in original wording.
- Formulas are specified for engine implementation but should be validated against a current edition source and, where financial, against the platform's existing FCAC-validated engine.
- Legal/regulatory statements are informational, not legal advice; the four pending consultations (SaaS, securities, real estate regulatory, tech E&O with AI rider) should confirm the term-protection, licensee-exposure, and AVM-scope conclusions before launch.
- The CRA "FMV inclusive of GST/HST" instruction to its appraisers comes from an internal 2023 communiqué reported by major accounting firms (PwC Canada, Baker Tilly), not a published public bulletin — treat as reliable but secondhand.
- Provincial rules vary; the report emphasizes BC and Alberta given the founder's licence and the platform's initial data coverage (Vancouver/Calgary/Edmonton), with Ontario (MPAC/HST) noted where relevant. The federal FTHB GST rebate is enacted; a proposed Ontario HST top-up (potentially up to a combined ~$130,000) was, at the time of research, an announcement/proposal only and should not be modelled as law.
